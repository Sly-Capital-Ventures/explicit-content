# Brand Voice

## Who's reading
Researchers and lab buyers sourcing reference-grade compounds — **not** consumers looking for a result. Write to someone who wants accurate identity, specs, and proof of quality, not persuasion.

## The voice
- **Precise, factual, neutral-scientific.** Spec-sheet clarity. Think supplier catalog crossed with a PubChem entry.
- **Calm and plain.** No hype, no urgency, no lifestyle framing, no persuading anyone toward *use*.
- **Confident about the product, silent about the body.** We're proud of purity, testing, COAs, selection, and shipping — never of an effect.

## Do
- State what a compound *is*, its chemistry, its research *class*.
- Highlight quality signals: third-party tested, COA available, lyophilized vials, sizes, in stock.
- Keep sentences declarative and specific. Real numbers (mg sizes, MW, CAS) over adjectives.

## Don't
- No benefit/effect/outcome language, however soft (see `compliance.md`).
- No dosing, protocols, "how to use", or research-as-instruction.
- No hype words ("game-changing", "powerful", "premium-grade miracle"), no fake scarcity, no testimonials.

## Brand facts
- Name: **Explicit** (research-compound supplier). RUO framing is always present.
- Support: **support@explicitresearch.com**.
- Everything is **for laboratory and research use only** — carry that framing without making it the whole sentence.

## The tone test
Read any sentence and ask: *"Is this a fact about the molecule or the product, or a suggestion about what it does for someone?"* If it's the second, rewrite or cut. Neutral and accurate always beats persuasive.
